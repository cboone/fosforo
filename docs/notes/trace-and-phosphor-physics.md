# Trace and phosphor physics

The beam's geometry, the phosphor's fade, and the several ways the display can show something the signal does not contain. Read this before judging whether what is on screen is the signal or an artifact. [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-renderer-simulates-a-crt.md) and [ADR 0017](../adr/0017-absolute-vertical-axis.md) are the decisions these implement.

- **The phosphor's fade is a time constant, and three things about how it reaches the shader are easy to get backwards.** `palette.decayOver(elapsed)` is `exp(-dt / tau)` with `tau` at 158.19 ms, derived at comptime from the pair `(60 Hz, 0.90)` so the anchor is the thing to argue with rather than a decimal. **`Renderer.frame` takes an absolute clock reading, never an interval**, because it is the only thing that knows which ticks committed: six early returns can skip one, and the time a skipped tick spanned is owed to whichever frame draws next, so the clock advances beside `accum_source` after `commit` and nowhere else. Hoisting that line above the semaphore wait was planted and it does not fail the decay check — it fails `CoreNotWhite`, because a retry after `.no_frame_slot` then sees a zero interval, nothing fades, and thirty deposits pile up to 29.703 against 9.539 while the white point stays where it was. **The first frame of a renderer's life stands in one interval at the reference rate rather than zero**, which looks like the fussier choice and is not: the fade is a no-op on a cleared pair either way, but zero sends `whitePoint` to its 8e5 clamp for that frame, which `smoke-trace` caught at two levels off its prediction and no eye would have. **That 8e5 is now a pinned figure rather than an arithmetic accident** ([#96](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/96)): the clamp's floor is `palette.min_dwell`, restated in `shaders/scope.metal` and `scripts/measure-trace` and pinned in both constants tests, and `zig build test` holds `whitePoint(1.0)` at 8e5 as a literal. Deriving that expectation from `white_headroom / min_dwell` would move with the constants it guards, which is why it is written out. Before it existed, moving the floor from 1e-6 to 1e-5 in all three languages at once left the suite at 285 of 285 passing. And the `exp` is computed once per frame on the CPU rather than once per pixel in MSL, which is what `AccumUniforms` carrying a decay rather than a `dt` and a `tau` buys.

- **The clock is `display_link.monotonicNanos()` and `CVTimeStamp` was refused, which is the reverse of what `src/platform/displaylink.zig` promised for two phases.** The argument is in that file and is not "the cheap one won": exponential decay composes, `exp(-(a + b) / tau) = exp(-a / tau) * exp(-b / tau)`, so the total fade depends on how long an interval was and not on how it was cut into frames. Both clocks are monotonic and both sum to the same wall time, so `output_time` buys a better subdivision on a frame that misses its deadline — at most one refresh period of phase error against a 158 ms constant, absorbed by the next interval rather than accumulated — against the cost of laying out 80 bytes of `CVTimeStamp` with a nested `CVSMPTETime` by hand, checked by nothing, where a wrong offset yields a plausible `dt` and a phosphor at the wrong speed. `src/gpu/palette.zig` holds the composition property as a test, so the premise is executable rather than asserted.

- **`zig build smoke-trace` is the only instrument that can see frame-rate independence, and the reason no screenshot can is structural.** Every other case there measures one frame, or a run at one rate; this is a claim about how successive frames _differ_, which is why #56's body expected it to be checked by pinning a display to 60 Hz in System Settings and judging a fade by eye. `checkDecayIsInRealTime` fades the same 96 ms as twelve 8 ms steps and as six 16 ms ones and asserts the two agree: 0.5430 and 0.5439 against a predicted 0.5451, 0.18% apart, where a per-frame factor would give 0.2824 and 0.5314. **Two traps if you touch it.** The span is 96 ms rather than 100 so both arms divide it into whole nanoseconds and cover the identical interval. And the expectation is composed per step rather than asked of the span, because `palette.max_elapsed_nanos` is 41.7 ms and `decayOver(96 ms)` clamps: it predicts 0.7684 against a true 0.5451, which is the first thing this check caught and it was in the check itself.

- **Metal has no line-width API, which is why the beam is geometry rather than a line.** Verified against the SDK headers rather than assumed: no `lineWidth`, no `setLineWidth`, nothing equivalent anywhere in `Metal.framework`. That is why the phase 2 trace was one device pixel, half a point on a 2x drawable, thin and jagged. #57 fixed it the only way ADR 0007 permits: each inter-sample segment is an instanced quad and the fragment shades it by distance from the segment, so the width is **3.0 points** and the antialiasing is the profile. The three wrong ways are still wrong and are recorded so they are not re-proposed: sub-pixel redraws triple the draw and stay aliased; MSAA needs `sampleCount` on the pipeline, the layer and the attachment plus a resolve, and ADR 0007 wants analytic antialiasing _instead_; and a line-width extension does not exist to be asked for.

- **The rail sits inside the drawable, and the reason changed at #57 while the value did not.** It used to be the rasterizer's coverage diamond: a one-pixel line centred on the drawable's boundary has half its diamond off-screen and may light nothing, so a railed signal would read as an absent one, which is the worst possible way for a level indication to fail. A quad has area and does not inherit that. What `iface.trace_rail`'s 1% now has to hold is the **beam's own half-width**, since a profile centred on the boundary loses whatever falls outside and the centroid `src/gpu/measure.zig` reads is dragged inward. The arithmetic is better than it was, because it is now scale-free: 1.5 points of half-width against `(1 - 0.98) * 135`, or 2.7 points, at the smallest editor `clampSize` permits, both in points, so the display's scale cancels instead of the guarantee holding separately at each one. `src/clap/gui.zig` holds the test and its bound moved with the reason.

  **The centre-line half pixel is answered, and the answer is that no bias was ever needed.** #38 measured silence in REAPER drawing a flat line on row 539 rather than 540 at 1920x1080, reading back as `+0.0021`, and declined the `+1.0/H` NDC bias on three grounds — two of which #57 falsified, since `Renderer` now stores its size and this is the step that re-answered the question. It turned out the geometry was never off: the _estimator_ was reading the beam's top edge. A symmetric profile centred on the boundary between two rows weights them equally, so its energy-weighted centroid is that boundary exactly, and `zig build smoke-trace` now reads **centroid row 269.500, implying a sample of 0.00000** at 960x540. Nothing was biased and nothing needed to be. What would still be worth investigating is the line _flickering_ between rows during playback, which would be a tie-break going unstable; that was watched for and did not happen.

- **The trace has a floor, and it is arithmetic.** One backing pixel of excursion needs a sample of `1 / (trace_full_scale * height / 2)`: about -54 dBFS at the default editor on a 2x display, about -42 dBFS at the smallest editor on a 1x one. Below that a sine moves the trace less than a pixel and reads as flat. It is the bottom of any level sweep and it is the price of ADR 0017's fixed axis, paid knowingly.

- **It has a ceiling too, and above 48 kHz the display invents structure rather than losing it.** The 20 ms window holds `0.02 * sample_rate` samples: 960 at 48 kHz, against a 960-point default editor, which is why [#37](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/37) chose that length and recorded that anything above it would alias. At 96 kHz it is 1920 samples, which on a 1x display at the default width is two per pixel and four in an editor dragged to its 480-point minimum. Every one of them is drawn, so what reaches the screen is whichever the rasterizer's coverage happens to catch. **On periodic material that is moiré, and moiré looks exactly like a real low-frequency component** — the display asserting something the signal does not contain, which is the same category of failure ADR 0017 refuses on the vertical axis rather than a cosmetic roughness. [#62](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/62) is min/max decimation and is the fix; `src/dsp/decimate.zig` has been reserved for it since phase 0 and does not exist yet. Until it lands, **verify at 48 kHz**, and treat any structure that appears only at a higher rate as suspect before believing it.

- **Railing is invisible at the level where it starts, and looking for a flat top is the wrong test.** Clamping begins at `trace_rail / trace_full_scale`, which is 1.0889 or +0.74 dBFS, and at exactly that level it removes a tenth of a backing pixel of travel at the default editor. A plateau reaches one pixel deep at 1.091 and five at 1.100; the sine that loses 443 pixels and looks obviously clipped is at 2.0. **What railing actually shows is the peak ceasing to climb:** 1.000 peaks 43 px below the rail, 1.050 peaks 19 px below it, and every level from 1.089 up peaks on exactly the same row. That saturation is the whole of what ADR 0017 means by declining to say how far over the signal is, and a level sweep that watches for a flat top instead of a stalled peak will report a defect that is not one. A saw is the sharper probe at small overshoots, because it crosses the rail on a linear ramp rather than at a turning point.

- **Stopping the transport used to draw a bright vertical line that lingered for about a second, and it was the signal rather than a defect.** [#58](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/58) removed it; the mechanism is kept here because the line was never a bug and the reasoning is what stops it being reintroduced. A host does not stop calling `process` when the transport stops; it keeps the audio device running and hands over silence. So the ring holds a **step** from the last sample to zero, one inter-sample interval wide and up to the full drawable tall, and the beam draws it faithfully. Two things made it prominent rather than subtle, and neither was a bug. Every segment used to deposit the same energy per pixel whatever its screen length, so a segment spanning 500 rows lit all 500 as brightly as an informative slow one. And #60's move to `BGRA8Unorm_sRGB` made the toe 12.92 times steeper, so a single deposit stays above green 16 for 54 frames rather than 31, roughly doubling visible persistence. **[#58](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/58) removed the first of those and [#79](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/79) closes as covered rather than fixed**: a full-height segment now deposits **0.0034** against a dwelling trace's **1.57**, roughly 460 times dimmer, which is about green 15 over a background of 5, and stopping the transport in REAPER confirmed the line is gone. Do not write a rule to suppress it. Distinct from a _stale_ window, which is [#53](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/53) and looks nothing like this: there the picture stops changing, here it changes correctly and the change is ugly.

- **The beam is velocity weighted, and that is ADR 0007's "single relationship"** ([#58](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/58)). Each segment's deposit is multiplied by `h / (h + len)`, `h` the beam's half-width and `len` its screen length in backing pixels, so energy per unit length falls as `1 / len` above the beam's own width and levels off below it. **The constant is derived rather than chosen**: a capsule's integral of the biweight is `(16/15)·h·len + (π/3)·h²`, so that weight holds a segment's _total_ deposit constant to within 2%, which is the physical statement that a segment carries one sample interval of beam time whatever it does with it. It also means there is no epsilon anywhere, because `h + len ≥ h > 0`, and the floor's real job is physical rather than numerical: a beam moving less than its own width does not reduce a pixel's dwell. `zig build smoke-trace` drives four slopes across two sample densities and finds the total flat to **0.94%**, against a factor of **197** unweighted.

  **What it bought is discrimination between signals, not the order of magnitude four documents predicted.** ADR 0019 held `white_headroom` provisional on the claim that this would widen the dwell ratio tenfold, inferred from a 100 Hz sine. Measured offscreen, turning point against zero crossing at 0.5: 100 Hz goes **1.36 to 1.84** and 1 kHz goes **1.34 to 7.50**. A 100 Hz sine's fastest crossing is only 1.88 times the speed of its turning point, since the sweep runs at 96,000 px/s against the trace's 152,681 px/s, so nothing could widen it and 1.84 is the beam being honest. Before, a slow tone and a fast one both read about 1.35 and the display said the same thing about them, which is what a plot does. **Anyone re-deriving this from a single test tone will reach the same wrong conclusion; use two tones a decade apart.**

  **`white_headroom` is settled at 0.8 and the re-judgement moved to phase 4.** Judged in REAPER at 0.4 and 1.2 there is no visible difference, and that is arithmetic rather than eyesight: a moving trace deposits 1.57 once and slides on while white sits at 15.6 at 120 Hz, ten times further. Nothing between "moving" and "stationary" exists on a free-running sweep, so a white core on moving material is a property of a _triggered_ display. Lowering the headroom does not bridge it: at 0.2 a 100 Hz turning point still reaches only green 214, and the value that whitens it whitens everything. The only thing the constant changes is how long a cleared, dwelling pixel takes to whiten, 47 ms against 229 ms, which is over before it can be looked at.

  **It exposed two constants tied to the wrong thing, both filed.** `TraceUniforms.density` is the segment pitch rather than the time a segment stands for, so it ties brightness to the editor's width while this term responds to the same geometry in the other direction; above one sample per point it clamps and stops compensating entirely, taking a flat trace from 1.20 in a 960-point editor to **0.43** in a 3840-point one ([#125](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/125)). And `scripts/measure-trace`'s lit threshold is an absolute energy, so the fast parts of a trace now read as dark there, which breaks `--periods` and the lit-column counts while leaving the peak centroids every published table rests on untouched ([#126](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/126)).
