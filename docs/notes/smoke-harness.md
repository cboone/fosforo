# The smoke harness

`src/smoke.zig` is the only thing here that runs a Metal or AppKit call rather than type-checking one, and it is an executable rather than a test for reasons [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-gui-smoke-harness-as-a-build-step.md) records. Read this before changing the harness or any `zig build smoke-*` step.

- **The background is indistinguishable from a black window by eye, so do not use your eyes on it.** The drawable shows byte `RGB(5, 5, 8)` wherever no energy has landed: correct for a phosphor scope's background, and identical to failure at a glance. **Since #60 that value is not a literal in the shader at all** — it is entry zero of every gradient in `src/gpu/palette.zig`, which is that issue's own question about whether the background stays a literal, answered by construction. The drawable is `BGRA8Unorm_sRGB`, so what the palette holds is the linear value `5 / (255 * 12.92)` and the hardware encodes it on write; `checkResolve` asserts the byte the picture actually shows equals the palette's value at zero energy, so the two cannot drift. It inherited that literal from `clear_fragment`, which #55 deleted along with the pass it owned, and keeping the exact value is what keeps `scripts/measure-trace`'s crop working. Two checks do work. Launch the host from a terminal and read the once-a-second `rendering at N Hz` line that debug builds emit, which is the only positive statement that frames are being presented. Or screenshot the editor and sample a pixel: the giveaway is the blue channel sitting two or three above red and green, mirroring the shader's `0.03` against `0.02`, which neither an unrendered layer nor a host's own background reproduces. **That check works at the background and nowhere else,** for a reason established later and recorded below: a screenshot carries the display's colour space rather than the framebuffer's, and the background is the one value where that conversion is identity. `zig build smoke-appkit` asserts the same thing automatically through `Editor.framesPresented`, which exists because this deliverable cannot be seen (ADR 0013). [#45](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/45) proposed changing the colour so that looking would prove something, and was closed as subsumed by [#38](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/38) on the grounds that the trace is what makes the render visible, so the background does not have to be. **The trace over it did not retire this bullet, it moved it.** A trace stuck flat at zero looks correct against silence, and a window frozen several seconds ago looks identical to a live one against steady material, so "I can see a waveform" is still not the check. The two that separate those cases are in the plan's verification section: stop the transport and confirm the trace goes flat within a frame or two, and count periods against the known 20 ms window rather than judging the shape.

- **`zig build smoke-trace` is what answers "what did the pixels become", and #55 is why it exists.** The accumulation shipped with a resolve gain of `1 - decay`, which normalises a _stationary_ trace to the colour the unaccumulated line strip drew. The arithmetic is correct and the case it optimises is the wrong one: a sliding trace never lights the same pixel twice, so it never accumulates, so the gain divides every real signal by ten. A 100 Hz sine rendered as a black display, measured afterwards at peak green 53 against 255. **That passed 160 unit tests, `smoke-gpu`, `smoke-appkit`, `smoke-leaks`, `clap-validator` and the Metal validation layer**, and was found by eye in under a minute. `Editor.framesPresented` answers whether frames are presented, the counters answer whether resources balance, and `MTL_DEBUG_LAYER` answers whether the bindings are legal. [#51](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/51) added the check that answers the remaining question: replanted, that exact gain now fails `smoke-trace` with a channel 224 levels out.

  It renders through the shipping pipeline into a texture the backend owns, and it is **not** a readback of the drawable, which ADR 0013 refuses because `setFramebufferOnly:` is a property of the shipping renderer. `Renderer` carries a `Surface` union, `init` and `initOffscreen` share every acquisition, and `frame` branches at exactly three points: where it takes a colour attachment, where it binds one, and whether it presents. Change any of those three and the offscreen path stops being the shipping path, which is the whole basis for believing its numbers.

  **It reads two surfaces and the pairing is the point.** The accumulation is linear and unclipped, so the geometry assertions survive #57, #58 and #60 with changed numbers rather than a rewritten instrument; the resolved picture is the only place a defect confined to the resolve can show. The resolve check compares the two against each other rather than against the beam's literal, so it assumes nothing about how many segments covered a pixel.

  **Since [#92](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/92) the deciding is not in this step at all.** `src/gpu/verdict.zig` holds the fifteen judges the thirteen `check*` functions used to inline, names no Metal type, and is covered by `zig build test` on a runner with no graphics support. `src/smoke.zig` keeps the driving, and the seam is two value types: `measure.Image` over the accumulation and `verdict.Picture` over the resolved bytes. So a wrong _expectation_ now fails the unit suite, where before it could only be found by planting against a GPU, and the plant table in `docs/plans/done/2026-08-29-verify-the-shader-offscreen-against-the-constants.md` has a column naming the test that covers each row. **What the step prints is unchanged**, byte for byte, which is the whole test of that move; the judges print through a `say` gated on `!builtin.is_test`, so they stay silent in a test binary for the reason ADR 0013 gives.

  **Two things planting the judges established that reading them did not.** A blank readback used to pass the decay checks, because they divided by a first peak nothing checked and `nan > 0.02 * want` is **false**; a run that drew nothing at all reported a healthy fade. And `expectClose`'s reported defect was not one: #92 filed it as undefined at zero, where it in fact reduced to exact equality, which is correct; the real fault was that it read its tolerance's scale from one of its two arguments, so swapping them could change the verdict. Both are recorded in ADR 0013's #92 amendment, along with the rule they suggest: **encoding a plant is not the same as covering it**, since two arms survived their own weakening by sitting far enough outside a bound to be caught by something else.

  **It has not replaced a host.** It measures one frame of a window it supplied itself, so it says nothing about the audio path, the ring, the display link, or whether a host's editor is live: a trace stuck flat and a window frozen five seconds ago both pass it. The host procedure in [#38](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/38) is still what covers those.

  **Its waits are wall-clock, and [#89](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/89) is why that is a stated rule rather than an assumption.** Offscreen there is no display link, so a tight run of frames hits the three-deep semaphore on the fourth and `.no_frame_slot` is the ordinary case rather than an overload signal; `driveFrame` waits it out. That wait was bounded by 100,000 yields, on a docstring arguing that "a hundred thousand yields is many seconds of slack on a loaded runner", and it turned `main` red on a runner with nothing wrong with the shader: the failing step ran three seconds against four for a passing one, so it gave up faster than a healthy run finishes. **Measured with `frame` planted to report `.no_frame_slot` unconditionally: 14,468,498 attempts fit inside 2000 ms**, so the old bound was worth about **13.8 ms** on this machine, off by a factor of 150. The rule that generalises, for whoever adds the next wait to that file: **a sleeping wait may sum its sleeps and a yielding wait must read a clock.** `waitForFrames` and `waitForReload` accumulate a nominal poll interval soundly, because `sleep` guarantees a floor per turn and the sum therefore under-counts real elapsed time; `std.Thread.yield()` returns almost immediately when nothing else on the core is runnable and guarantees nothing at all. **Raising the count would have been the wrong repair**, which the same run shows: exposure is not proportional to frame count, since `checkHotCore` drives thirty frames against `checkDecay`'s five, runs before it, and passed. And the failure message now names the measured elapsed rather than the ceiling, because under the old bound a give-up at two seconds and one at forty milliseconds printed identically.

- **`zig build` does not rebuild the smoke harness, and running a stale one wastes an afternoon.** `build.zig` installs `fosforo-smoke` with `b.addInstallArtifact` attached to the smoke steps rather than with `b.installArtifact`, deliberately, so the day-to-day loop keeps producing only the `.clap`. The consequence is that `zig build && zig-out/bin/fosforo-smoke appkit 40` runs whatever binary a _previous_ smoke step left there, with no warning and no hash to compare. This is the provenance hazard the plug-in folder has, one directory closer to home. Run a smoke step, or run `zig build smoke-gpu` first if what you want is the harness rebuilt without waiting for the AppKit half.

- **The watcher polls, and each editor's cycle in `smoke-appkit` is shorter than one poll interval.** 250 ms between looks, against a cycle in `oneCycle` lasting about as long, so a watcher started on an editor's first frame is joined before it ever looks twice: an arm testing reload from inside `oneCycle` cannot work and reads as the watcher being broken. That is why `hotReloadPhase` holds one editor open instead, once per process. Polling rather than a `dispatch_source_t` on the file descriptor is also deliberate: such a source watches a vnode, and the save-by-rename case the three-field stamp exists for is exactly where the watched vnode stops being the path.

- **The smoke harness is an executable, and that is not an oversight.** `src/smoke.zig` is the only thing here that runs a Metal or an AppKit call rather than type-checking one. It is not a test artifact because a test binary has to stay silent: `std.debug.print` from inside one interleaves with the runner's stream and the build runner reports the step as failed despite a zero exit code, which is the same hazard `src/clap/log.zig` disables its `stderr` mirror under `builtin.is_test` to avoid. And it is never wired into `zig build test`, on `validate-shaders`' precedent, because the default test path must not acquire a dependency on the machine having a GPU. It is also the only check in the project that can go red for reasons unrelated to the code, which is why the gating in CI is not uniform: `smoke-gpu`, `smoke-trace` and, since [#72](https://github.com/cboone/fosforo/issues/72), `smoke-appkit` all fail the job, while `smoke-leaks` stays under `continue-on-error` (ADR 0013). **That asymmetry is the argument rather than a leftover.** `smoke-appkit` fails on its own assertions plus the window-server connection 65 green runs settled, both of them facts about this repository; `smoke-leaks` also judges the runner's own AppKit chatter, against class prefixes including `NSView`, `CALayer` and `IOSurface`, so a framework leaking on its own account fails it with nothing here being wrong. A second consequence of the promotion is that an `appkit` failure now _skips_ the leak step rather than running it, since nothing there carries an `if:`, which is `scripts/smoke-leak-check`'s own first assertion enforced one level up. **`smoke-leaks` runs there too**, at 40 cycles through `-Dleak-cycles`, since it is the `appkit` half with an instrument wrapped around it. Its criteria do not track depth — across five runs at 40 and at 400 cycles the report gave 288 leaks for 18,816 bytes four times and 285 for 18,464 once, so a tenfold change in depth moves it by under 2% and only run to run — while its cost does, because the harness waits on vsync about thirteen times per cycle and a hosted runner refreshes at half this machine's rate. Its `main` takes a `std.process.Init.Minimal`, which is how Zig 0.16 hands over argv; there is no `std.process.args()` any more.
